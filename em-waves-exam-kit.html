Topic 1 · exam Q7

Induced electric field (Faraday's law)

A changing magnetic flux creates a circulating electric field, even in empty space with no wire.

\[\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec l = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad E\,(2\pi r) = \pi r^2\,\frac{dB}{dt}\]

The method

  1. Draw a circle of radius \(r\) through point P, centred on the axis (symmetry makes \(E\) constant on it).
  2. Left side: \(E \cdot 2\pi r\).
  3. Right side: rate of flux through that circle. Inside the solenoid the area is \(\pi r^2\); outside it is only \(\pi R^2\) (field exists only inside).
  4. Use \(B = \mu_0 n i\), so \(dB/dt = \mu_0 n\,di/dt\). Solve for \(E\).
Inside \((r<R)\): \[E = \frac{\mu_0 n r}{2}\,\frac{di}{dt}\]
Outside \((r>R)\): \[E = \frac{\mu_0 n R^2}{2r}\,\frac{di}{dt}\]
Traps
  • Only the rate \(di/dt\) matters. A constant part of the current (\(h_0\)) produces no induced field.
  • For a point inside, the solenoid radius \(R\) is not needed. "Cannot be determined" is a distractor.
  • \(\mu_0 n k \pi r^2\) is the flux rate, not the field. You still have to divide by \(2\pi r\).
Lab · field profile E(r)
EXAM Q7 Solenoid with \(i = h_0 + kt\)

An infinite solenoid with \(n\) turns per metre carries \(i = h_0 + kt\). Find \(E\) at distance \(r\) from the axis, inside.

\(di/dt = k\) (the \(h_0\) drops out). Faraday on a circle of radius \(r\):

\[E\,2\pi r = \pi r^2 \mu_0 n k \;\Rightarrow\; E = \frac{\mu_0 n k r}{2}\]
Answer: \(E = \dfrac{\mu_0 n k r}{2}\)
Topic 2 · exam Q9

Mutual and self inductance

Inductance is a geometry number. It tells you how much flux one circuit gets per ampere in the other.

\[M = \frac{\Phi_{21}}{i_1} = \frac{\Phi_{12}}{i_2}\qquad \varepsilon_2 = -M\frac{di_1}{dt}\]
\[L = \frac{\Phi}{i},\quad L_{sol} = \mu_0 n^2 A\ell,\quad U = \tfrac12 L i^2\]
M or L depends onM or L does NOT depend on
Shape and size of each circuitThe current flowing
Distance between circuitsThe rate of change of current
Relative orientationTime
Medium in between (its \(\mu\))The induced emf
Number of turnsWhich circuit you call "1" (\(M_{12}=M_{21}\))
Why the current never matters

Flux is proportional to the current, so the ratio \(\Phi/i\) cancels it. Same logic as capacitance \(C = Q/V\), which does not depend on \(Q\).

EXAM Q9 Mutual induction does NOT depend on…

Options: shape of circuits, distance, current intensity, medium. Shape, distance and medium all change how flux links the circuits. Current only scales flux and cancels in \(M=\Phi/i\).

Answer: the intensity of current flowing in the two circuits.
Topic 3 · exam Q14

Displacement current

Maxwell's term: a changing electric field acts like a current. If E inside a capacitor does not change, there is no displacement current.

\[I_d = \varepsilon_0\frac{d\Phi_E}{dt},\qquad J_d = \varepsilon_0\frac{\partial E}{\partial t},\qquad E_{cap} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0 A}\]

The method

  1. Ask: what is held fixed? Charge \(Q\) (isolated) or voltage \(V\) (battery connected)?
  2. Write \(E\) using what's fixed: isolated → \(E = Q/(\varepsilon_0 A)\); battery → \(E = V/d\).
  3. Check if \(E\) changes with time. \(J_d \propto dE/dt\).
SituationE insideDisplacement current
Isolated capacitor, plates pulled apart\(Q/(\varepsilon_0 A)\): constantZero
Battery connected, plates pulled apart\(V/d\): decreasesNon-zero
Capacitor charging with current \(I\)grows\(I_d = I\) (continuity)

EXAM Q14 Isolated charged capacitor, plates separating

Isolated means \(Q\) cannot change. Ignoring edge effects, \(E = \sigma/\varepsilon_0 = Q/(\varepsilon_0 A)\), which does not contain \(d\). So \(E\) is constant while the plates move, and \(J_d = \varepsilon_0\,\partial E/\partial t = 0\).

Answer: is zero.
Topic 4 · exam Q10

Magnetic field of a moving charge

Biot–Savart for one charge. Learn which "distance law" goes with which source; examiners swap them.

\[\vec B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\,\frac{q\,\vec v\times\hat r}{r^2}\qquad |B| = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{|q| v\sin\theta}{r^2}\]

So \(B\) is proportional to \(q\), to \(v\), depends on direction through \(\sin\theta\) (zero straight ahead or behind), and falls as \(1/r^2\).

SourceField falls as
Point charge (E) / moving point charge (B)\(1/r^2\)
Infinite straight wire (B) / infinite line charge (E)\(1/r\)
Infinite plane / inside ideal solenoidconstant
Dipole (electric or magnetic), far away\(1/r^3\)
Point-source EM wave: intensity / amplitude\(1/r^2\) / \(1/r\)
EXAM Q10 Field of a moving (non-relativistic) charge

Check each option: depends on direction (through \(\vec v\times\hat r\)) so "does not depend on direction" is false; depends on \(q\) so "does not depend on charge" is false; \(1/r\) is the wire law, not the point-charge law.

Answer: inversely proportional to the square of the distance.
Topic 5 · exam Q5

Solutions of the wave equation

\(\dfrac{\partial^2\xi}{\partial x^2} = \dfrac{1}{v^2}\dfrac{\partial^2\xi}{\partial t^2}\) is solved by any shape that slides at speed \(v\).

\[\xi(x,t) = f(x - vt) + g(x + vt)\]

The 5-second test

  1. Look inside every function. Is the argument built only from the combination \(x - vt\) (or only \(x + vt\))?
  2. A constant multiple is fine: \(f[2(x-vt)]\), \(\sin(kx - \omega t) = \sin[k(x - vt)]\) with \(v=\omega/k\).
  3. Squares on only one variable break it: \(x^2 - vt\), \(x - vt^2\), \(x^2 - vt^2\) are not waves.
  4. Sums of valid pieces are valid (the equation is linear).
Drill · wave or not?
EXAM Q5 Which is a solution?

\(f_1(x^2 - vt^2)\) ✗, \(f_1(x - vt^2) + f_2(x + vt^2)\) ✗, \(f_1(x^2 - vt)\) ✗, \(f_1(x - vt) + f_2[2(x - vt)]\) ✓. The last one: both pieces are functions of \(u = x - vt\) only.

Answer: \(\xi = f_1(x - vt) + f_2[2(x - vt)]\)
Topic 6 · exam Q6, Q12

Wavelength, frequency and speed

In vacuum every EM wave travels at \(c\). Short wavelength means high frequency.

\[f = \frac{c}{\lambda}\qquad 1\ \text{nm} = 10^{-9}\ \text{m}\]
Lab · visible light
Traps
  • "Highest frequency" means use the shortest wavelength (400 nm, violet).
  • Forgetting the \(10^{-9}\) gives answers like \(3\times10^{8}\) or \(10^{20}\) Hz. Visible light is always about \(4\text{–}7.5\times10^{14}\) Hz; use that as a sanity check.
  • Speed in vacuum is the same for all colours. Any option saying red is "faster" or "slower" in vacuum is wrong.
EXAM Q6 Highest visible frequency

\(f_{max} = c/\lambda_{min} = 3.0\times10^8 / 400\times10^{-9} = 7.5\times10^{14}\) Hz.

Answer: \(7.5\times10^{14}\) Hz
EXAM Q12 Red (700 nm) vs violet (400 nm)

Red: longer λ, so lower frequency. Same speed \(c\). The options all claim different speeds or higher frequency, so none fits.

Answer: None of the others.
Topic 7 · exam Q8, Q11

Linking E and B: E = cB

In a plane EM wave in vacuum, E and B are in phase, perpendicular, and their magnitudes are locked together.

\[E = cB\qquad B = \frac{E}{c}\qquad (E_0 = cB_0\ \text{for amplitudes too})\]
Unit traps
  • N/C and V/m are the same unit.
  • \(1\ \mu\text{T} = 10^{-6}\ \text{T}\). Typical answers: hundreds of N/C ↔ a few µT.
  • If you get \(\mu\text{N/C}\) for E, you divided by \(c\) instead of multiplying.

\(B = 570 / 3.0\times10^8 = 1.90\times10^{-6}\) T.

Answer: 1.90 µT

Topic 8 · exam Q3, Q4, Q13, Q15 · 4 of 15 questions

Intensity and the Poynting vector

The highest-value topic on this test. Learn the chain: power → intensity → amplitude.

\[S_{max} = \frac{E_0B_0}{\mu_0} = \frac{cB_0^2}{\mu_0} = \frac{E_0^2}{\mu_0 c}\qquad I = \langle S\rangle = \frac{S_{max}}{2} = \frac{E_0^2}{2\mu_0 c}\]

The chain for point sources

  1. Isotropic source of power \(P\): energy spreads over a sphere, so \(I = P/(4\pi r^2)\).
  2. Invert \(I = E_0^2/(2\mu_0 c)\): \(E_0 = \sqrt{2\mu_0 c\, I} \approx \sqrt{754\, I}\).
  3. If they ask for \(B_0\): divide by \(c\).

Energy on a surface

  1. "Average Poynting vector" = intensity \(I\) (already averaged, no extra ½).
  2. \(U = I \times A \times \Delta t\). Convert minutes to seconds. If the surface is tilted, multiply by \(\cos\theta\).
Traps
  • "Maximum" Poynting → no ½. "Average" or "intensity" → with ½.
  • Sphere area is \(4\pi r^2\), not \(4\pi r\) or \(\pi r^2\).
  • Using \(I = E_0^2/(\mu_0 c)\) (no 2) gives the rms field \(E_0/\sqrt2\): a classic wrong option.
Calculator · point source
EXAM Q4 1500 W at 3 m

\(I = 1500 / (4\pi\cdot 9) = 13.26\) W/m². \(E_0 = \sqrt{2\cdot 377\cdot 13.26} = \sqrt{10\,000} = 100\) V/m.

Answer: 100 V/m
EXAM Q13 240 W at 2 m

\(I = 240 / (4\pi\cdot 4) = 4.77\) W/m². \(E_0 = \sqrt{754\cdot 4.77} = \sqrt{3600} = 60\) V/m.

Answer: 60 V/m
EXAM Q3 Energy on a 1.5 m × 2.0 m area in 1 minute

\(\langle S\rangle = 0.939\) W/m² is already the average. \(U = 0.939 \times (1.5\times2.0) \times 60 = 169\) J.

Answer: 170 J
EXAM Q15 \(B_0 = 2\times10^{-6}\) T, find \(S_{max}\)

\(S_{max} = cB_0^2/\mu_0 = 3\times10^8 \cdot 4\times10^{-12} / (4\pi\times10^{-7}) = 955.8\) W/m². The option 478 is the average (half), a trap.

Answer: 955.8 W/m²
Topic 9 · exam Q1, Q2

Polarization

Follow the tip of \(\vec E\) at a fixed point. Its path is a line, a circle or an ellipse.

\[\vec E = E_1\cos(kz-\omega t)\,\hat x + E_2\cos(kz-\omega t+\varphi)\,\hat y\]

The method

  1. Read off the phase difference \(\varphi\) between the y and x components.
  2. \(\varphi = 0\): \(E_y/E_x = +E_2/E_1\), a line at \(\arctan(E_2/E_1)\). With \(E_1 = E_2\): 45°.
  3. \(\varphi = \pi\): \(E_y/E_x = -E_2/E_1\), a line at \(180^\circ - \arctan(E_2/E_1)\). With \(E_1 = E_2\): 135°.
  4. \(\varphi = \pm\pi/2\) and \(E_1 = E_2\): circle. Any other case: ellipse.
  5. Rotation sense: set \(z = 0\), check the tip at \(t=0\) and a moment later. With this form, looking from \(+z\) toward the origin, \(\varphi = +\pi/2\) turns counterclockwise and \(\varphi = -\pi/2\) clockwise.
Lab · trace the tip of E (seen from +z, looking at the origin)

Definition trap (Q2)
  • Linearly polarized = the plane of oscillation of \(\vec E\) (and of \(\vec B\)) stays fixed in time.
  • "E and B perpendicular to propagation" is true for every plane EM wave, so it does not define linear polarization.
  • E and B in vacuum are in phase, never out of phase by \(\pi/2\), and never parallel.
EXAM Q1 \(E_2 = E_1\), phase \(\pi\)

Real parts: \(E_x = E_1\cos(kz-\omega t)\), \(E_y = E_1\cos(kz-\omega t+\pi) = -E_x\). The tip moves along \(y = -x\): a straight line at 135° from +x.

Answer: straight at 135° with respect to the +x axis.
EXAM Q2 Definition of linear polarization
Answer: the plane of oscillation of the electric field and that of the magnetic field remain constant over time.
